I ran StoryForge in headless Chromium at 390×844, with React bundled locally and a mock standing in for the AI. Every screen and flow worked, with no console errors and no sideways scrolling. I did not test the real Claude call, since that only works inside the Claude viewer, and Export is simulated.

**What works:** the Home to Script to Storyboard to Timeline path, the motion editor with live animation, and AI tool calls that edit scenes and style.

**Where the build falls short (worst first):**
1. **The chat hides the work.** When the AI writes a script, the chat shows only a short reply, and you must open the scenes view to see what changed. The new project also kept its default title. AI edits should appear as reviewable cards with Undo.
2. **The art doesn't hold together.** Scene 3 drops the second character and the water cooler, and every Home thumbnail looks identical. Consistent characters are the product's core promise, and stick figures don't deliver it.
3. **The timeline is a toy.** It has no time ruler, playhead, drag-to-trim, or audio, and "Nar" is cryptic.
4. **Nothing persists, and export is fake.**
5. **Smaller issues:**
   - Project titles on Home are only 25px tall to tap.
   - The re-roll button covers part of each panel.
   - The same dice icon means both "re-roll" and "try again."
   - Reset and "Apply to all" are too faint.
   - The AI can't generate the storyboard or export for you.

**Against the field:** CapCut's own blog says it turns a script into a video, then lets you click "Edit More" to enter the online editor, and it claims script-to-video maker, auto captions, and AI avatars in one place. Descript is best known for editing through transcripts, the "edit like a doc" approach. InVideo makes a complete video from a text prompt, with voiceover, music, and transitions. Those sources are vendor-leaning roundups. What I say about Runway, Google's tools, and Vyond is from memory and unchecked.

- **Where they win:** raw generation quality, asset libraries, real export, and one-tap results.
- **Where you can win:** none of them owns timing for single-panel gag cartoons, which is your wedge.
- **Flow:** from Home, a first draft takes about six taps plus one prompt. That is comparable on taps, but they generate everything in one step and we don't yet.

**What I'd build next:**
1. Reviewable AI edits with Undo, plus a one-tap "Make video" that chains script, storyboard, and motion.
2. A character and prop sheet per project, so scenes stay consistent, with real generated art.
3. A timing engine that suggests punchline pauses and offers A/B caption variants.
4. A real timeline, saved projects, and real export.

I'd start with 1 and 4; they fix the most visible problems fastest. Should I?
