Here's how print gag cartoons get animated, and how your app could automate it.

**How studios animate this style**
- **Limited animation:** Keep the single-panel drawing mostly static and animate only small things: a blink, a hand gesture, a coffee sip. The New Yorker's animated cartoons work this way.
- **Line boil:** Redraw the outlines in 2–3 slightly different versions and cycle them, so the ink looks hand-drawn and alive. Hertzfeldt and Simon's Cat both lean on this.
- **Cutout rigs:** Split the drawing into layers (head, arm, speech bubble, background) and move them like paper puppets. This is how Mark Fiore does animated editorial cartoons.
- **Camera moves:** Slow push-ins, pans, and parallax on a flat drawing make it feel cinematic.
- **Punchline timing:** The caption appears after a beat of silence, as a typed reveal or a voice-over line. Timing carries the joke.

**Script-to-cartoon pipeline for your app**
1. **Script → beats:** Split the script into one-joke or one-idea panels.
2. **Style lock:** The user picks a look (New Yorker watercolor wash, Glasbergen bold doodle, loose pen line) and the app keeps characters consistent across panels.
3. **Panel generation:** Generate each panel as separate layers (characters, props, background) rather than one flat image. Layers are what make animation possible.
4. **Auto-motion:** Apply presets: line boil, blink, gesture loop, push-in, caption reveal.
5. **Voice and timing:** Add narration or caption timing, with an auto-pause before the punchline.
6. **Editable timeline:** The user can reorder panels, swap styles, and re-roll a single panel without redoing the whole video. This is where you beat a one-click "overview" generator.
