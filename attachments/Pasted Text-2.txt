Keynote's core idea is a simple hierarchy: a deck is a list of slides, each slide has a layout, and you can see that list three ways (navigator, outline, light table). That maps almost directly onto "script → scenes → video."

**What Keynote does**
- **Three working views:** Slide view shows one slide for editing, and Outline shows a text outline with slide thumbnails alongside. There's also a light table grid for overview.
- **Slide navigator:** a thumbnail strip beside the canvas. You tap a thumbnail to select it, hold and drag to reorder, and drag right to indent and group slides. Grouped slides can be collapsed.
- **Layouts:** Applying a different layout restyles a slide, and you can reapply the original layout later without deleting your content.
- **Skip instead of delete:** A skipped slide stays editable but doesn't play, which is handy for keeping two versions of a slide.
- **Toolbar:** a short top bar with Add, Format, Animate, and Play. On iPad it can be customized by dragging buttons on and off.
- **Play and share:** Playback advances by tap or by links only, and collaboration and animated GIF export are built in.

**What to borrow**

| Keynote | Your app |
|---|---|
| Slide | Scene or panel |
| Slide navigator | Scene strip, drag to reorder |
| Outline view | **Script view** (your hero feature) |
| Light table | Storyboard grid |
| Layouts | Cartoon templates (watercooler, podium, desk) |
| Skip slide | Skip or mute scene, keep alt takes |
| Grouped slides | Chapters or acts |
| Animate tab | Motion presets (line boil, blink, push-in) |
| Play / Rehearse | Preview with timing |

**Suggested flow and tabs (phone)**
1. **Home:** a project grid like Keynote's document manager, with an "Import script" button.
2. **Script:** edit text. The app splits it into scenes, and each line becomes a scene.
3. **Storyboard:** a grid of generated panels. Tap one to open it, and long-press to re-roll or swap style.
4. **Scene editor:** the canvas with a bottom toolbar of **Style · Motion · Voice · Timing**. This replaces Keynote's Format and Animate.
5. **Timeline:** scenes as thumbnails along a bar. Drag to reorder and trim.
6. **Preview and export:** play, then export as MP4 or GIF, or share.

One twist to Keynote: edits in the script should flow down to the storyboard automatically, so the user changes the words and the scenes update. That's your NotebookLM-style advantage.
